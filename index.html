Dr. Satyaghosh Maurya

I watch single proteins change shape, and time the moment they do.

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Gilad Haran Lab at the Weizmann Institute of Science. Proteins work by switching between conformations: an enzyme closes over its substrates and opens again, a transporter flips between inward- and outward-facing states. Each spends almost all of its time settled in one conformation or the other, and only microseconds actually crossing between them.

About

That crossing, the transition path, is where the mechanism lives. It is chemical reaction engineering at the scale of one molecule: a conformational change is a unimolecular reaction, the two conformations are wells on a potential, and the crossing is the barrier passage that rate theory, from Arrhenius to Kramers, has always described from the outside. A transition-path time measures that passage directly, reporting on the shape and roughness of the barrier in a way that rates, which only count how often crossings happen, cannot.

The obstacle is photons. A single FRET pair yields a few hundred photons per millisecond at best, so a microsecond crossing leaves a photon or two, too few for conventional smFRET to time. We fabricate zero-mode waveguides and plasmonic nanostructures that confine light to a zeptolitre volume, through which single molecules diffuse, and accelerate their emission; the photon stream is then treated as the noisy process data it is and fitted photon by photon with maximum-likelihood and Bayesian methods.

I trained as a chemical engineer, at NIT Durgapur and then at the Indian Institute of Science, where my Ph.D. followed pore-forming toxins assembling on membranes and proteins diffusing through grafted polymer layers: nucleation and growth, and transport at an interface, watched one molecule at a time. That training is the lens for everything above.

Core Research Themes

Bridging single-molecule optics, nanophotonics, statistical inference, and membrane biophysics.

Zero-Mode Waveguides & Nanophotonics

Overcoming the optical diffraction and concentration barrier using metallic nano-apertures (ZMWs) to study single-molecule interactions at micromolar, physiological concentrations.

Photon-by-Photon Inference & Image Analysis

H2MM, the photon-by-photon hidden Markov method developed in the Gilad Haran Lab, recovers conformational states, rates and transition-path times straight from photon arrival times, with no binning. From my Ph.D.: single-particle tracking and photobleaching-step counting on TIRF movies to measure mobility and oligomer size.

Awadh Biodiversity Atlas: community natural history Work in progress

An open-access citizen-science project, still in its early stage, to document the overlooked native flora, insects, birds and wetland life of rural eastern Uttar Pradesh, in the Basti and Ayodhya districts. Draft bilingual profiles for more than 330 species so far; field verification, photographs and dated observations from the region are the next stage. Working toward bilingual pictographic field guides and rural school science programmes.
