Prospective students

I am a postdoctoral researcher, so I am not taking students yet. This page is for anyone deciding whether to keep in touch, and it says what the laboratory will be like when it opens.

Where things stand

I am applying for faculty positions in chemical engineering in India. Until an appointment is made there is no group to join. I am still, in the ways that matter, a student myself, learning the trade in the Haran laboratory. What I can offer now is a reply: if you write to me about the work described on this site, I will answer, and I will tell you when there is a position to apply for.

What a first year would look like

The laboratory will build its own instruments and measure what single molecules do with them. A new student’s first months are taught, not left to chance: laser and chemical safety, programming and version control, and a way of organising data so a result can be found again two years later. The first project will usually have all three parts of the work in it, in miniature: build something (an optical path, a nanostructured chip, a bioconjugate), measure something with it, and analyse the photons or images that come out. By the end of the year a student should have made one measurement that nobody has made before, however small, and written it up.

Students leave with a set of skills that travel: single-molecule optics, nanofabrication and surface chemistry, statistical inference on noisy data, and clear writing. The Future Directions page describes the four lines of work a project would belong to.

Who fits

Chemical engineering, physics, chemistry and biotechnology backgrounds all fit. Programming helps; biology is not required and is taught as needed. The useful qualities are patience with an instrument that does not work on the first day, honesty about a result that disagrees with the plan, and the habit of writing things down.

Summer and project students

Summer projects are where many students first meet research, and students from state universities and smaller colleges rarely get the chance. A share of summer places will be reserved for them. The teaching laboratory described on the Teaching page, where students build a simple instrument and model what it measures, is open to project students as well.
